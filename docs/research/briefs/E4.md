# Brief D4: E4 amounts per placement (plan §4 E4, H2; theory plan §2)

Author: Opus design agent, 2026-10-31. Consumers: six Sonnet agents, one per placement, each turning
one section below into `scripts/research/specs/e4-<placement>.json`, a 10,000-session report and a
ledger fragment. Read `docs/research/HANDOFF.md` §3–§4 first. Every JSON below was smoke-run (300
sessions) through `npm run research:sim` and through a scratch harness script that counts
`rejected`/`adjusted` (results in §5).

## 1. What every agent does

1. Copy the JSON of your section verbatim into `scripts/research/specs/e4-<placement>.json`, run
   `npx prettier --write` on it. Do not change amounts, ids, seed or meta.
2. Check level 0: `npm run research:sim scripts/research/specs/e4-<placement>.json -- --sessions 300 --spins 1`.
   `wager/spin` must equal the **level-0 total** of your section for every experiment
   (one spin is always level 0). Anything else means the layout did not place as designed: stop and
   report.
3. Full run: `npm run research:sim scripts/research/specs/e4-<placement>.json -- --out docs/research/results/e4-<placement>.json`
   (10,000 sessions, seed 20261031, 500 spins; 20–40 s per experiment).
4. Ledger fragment `docs/research/ledger/e4-<placement>.md`, one row per experiment. Prediction
   cell: copy the "prediction" line of the experiment from §4 of this brief, then "→" and whether it
   held. Note cell: `onCap reset`, bankroll $5,000. Report median **beside** mean, CVaR 5 % and
   bust; check realized edge against −2.70 % (outside its 95 % CI = bug, say so loudly).
5. Also record, from the report's `path`, the share of sessions ahead at spins 100, 200, 300, 400,
   500 and the **first** spin at which `ahead` drops below 0.5 (see §6.2 for why
   `medianAheadUntil` alone is not enough here).

## 2. Design decisions (apply to all six)

**Base stake: $25 total on the table at level 0 for every placement** (two dozens $26 = $13 × 2,
35 straight-ups $35 = 35 × $1, the smallest legal amounts there). Reasons: (a) the inside
`minTotal` is $25, so a single six-line or corner cannot start below $25 and 25 straight-ups at $1
are exactly $25; (b) the outside minimum is $25 and martingale-red / flat-red in the ledger use
$25; (c) with one base and one bankroll, every placement has the same bankroll in base stakes
(≈ 200), so placement (p, g, the per-bet max) is the only variable, which is what H2 asks. The B/u
lever is tested separately by one extra pair in the dozen spec (`-u5`, $5 base, outside minimum
lowered to $5); the sweep over B/u itself is E3a's job.

**Amounts (the rounding rule).** Per-bet amount at level k:
`a_k = max(ceil_$1(g^k × a_0), smallest $1 multiple with net_k ≥ net_0)`, where
`net_k = a_k × (P + 1) − n × Σ_{j≤k} a_j` is the session net if the cycle ends with a win at level
k (P = the bet's payout, n = number of bets, all bets equal and disjoint), and `net_0` is the
level-0 profit. With exact g every level nets exactly `net_0`; rounding each level up does not
guarantee it (pure `ceil(g^k)` on 25 straight-ups gives 1, 4, 11 and level 2 nets **−$4**), so a
level is bumped (marked † in the tables) to the smallest chip amount that recovers every earlier
loss plus the level-0 profit. Every g ladder below therefore nets at least `net_0` and less than
`net_0` plus one chip's win (P + 1 − n dollars) at every level; the deficit column shows it.

**Levels L:** the largest L with `a_{L−1} ≤ per-bet max` and the full ladder
`n × Σ a_k ≤ $5,000` (a fresh bankroll can always pay a whole ladder). The binding limit is named
per experiment, with the level that would not fit.

**×2 control:** same base, `a_k = a_0 × 2^k`, L by the same rule. For c > 18 doubling
under-recovers: two dozens nets $0 at level 1 and below zero after, 25 and 35 straight-ups net below
zero from level 1. Those controls lose through negative cycles, not caps; `meta.recovers` is
`false` for them, their q and n½ are not meaningful, and E5 should leave them out of the e^(−x)
fit.

**Strategy shape.** One `sequence` progression `q`,
`{ kind: 'sequence', track: 'round', onWin: 'reset', onLoss: 'advance', onCap: 'reset', amounts }`,
whose amounts are **per bet**; every layout entry is `{ bet, amount: { prog: 'q' } }`. (Per-bet
amounts avoid `times` with a fraction like 1/25, which reconcile would floor below the chip.)
`track: 'round'` reads the round's net: for every layout here a winning spin has round net > 0
(P + 1 > n) and a losing spin < 0, so win = reset, loss = advance. `onCap: 'reset'` as in the
presets; state it in the ledger note.

**Numbers covered:** dozen 1; dozens 1 and 2; six-line 1–6; corner 1-2-4-5; straight-ups 1–25;
straight-ups 1–35 (0 and 36 uncovered). Which numbers does not matter on a fair wheel.

**Bankroll:** $5,000 for all (the plan default, and the ledger's comparison point). No other
bankroll is justified: the ladders are sized to it.

**Rules overrides:** only two dozens (outside min $13, since each dozen gets $13) and the dozen
`-u5` pair (outside min $5). Inside placements need none.

**meta** (read by the aggregator): `c`, `p = c/37`, `B` = full-ladder stake / level-0 stake (Σ g^k
after rounding), `q = (1 − p)^L`, plus `g`, `L`, `base` (level-0 total, cents) and `recovers`.

The model's "until" for the g ladders is fragile: P(ahead) dips below 0.5 around n½ and comes back
near 0.5 later (sessions with exactly one cap climb back above zero), so the last spin with
median > 0 can move by ±100 spins between seeds. Model P(ahead) at spins 100/200/300/400:
dozen-g .64/.41/.52/.44; two-dozens-g .70/.49/.35/.55; sixline-g .61/.41/.49/.39; corner-g
.59/.43/.46/.38; straights25-g .78/.61/.49/.42; straights35-g .75/.56/.43/.32; dozen-g-u5
.90/.83/.77/.71.

What E4 should show (H2): with the right g, every placement's horizon is in the low hundreds at
B ≈ 200 base stakes, against 14–33 spins for the over-recovering ×2 ladders (dozen, six-line,
corner). The dozen goes from n½ 33 (×2) to 159 (g = 1.5); with a $5 base (B 921) to 765 and a
positive median at 500. **Ranking:** at equal B the n½ order is 25 straight-ups (286) > 35
straight-ups (251, but only L = 2) > two dozens (200) > dozen (159) > six-line (147) > corner
(141), i.e. it grows with coverage. That is the *opposite* of "ranking follows B/(u·p)" in plan
§4 E4: since q ≈ m/B (m = (36 − c)/c, the payout per unit staked), n½ ≈ ln2 · B/(m·p) =
ln2 · 37 · B/(36 − c). The cap loss measured in level-0 *profits* (B/m), not in stakes, sets the
horizon. Suggest the orchestrator correct that sentence of the prediction before the runs land.

## 6. Flags for the orchestrator

1. **n½ is a first-cap horizon.** At n½ a ladder has accumulated about ln2 × (one ladder) of
   profit, so with `onCap: reset` and bankroll ≈ one ladder a session with exactly one cap is back
   above zero some spins later (dozen-g: after ≈ 267 spins, when 0.324 × S × $50 > $4,323). The
   median therefore drops below zero near n½ and can rise above it again; `medianAheadUntil`
   (last spin with median > 0) overstates the horizon (dozen-g model 340 vs n½ 159). For E5,
   compare n½ with the **first** spin where `ahead` < 0.5, from the report path. An `onCap: stop`
   variant would make the two coincide; not added here (E7 covers stops).
2. **The plan's E4 ranking sentence** ("follows B/(u·p)") should read B/(m·p) ≈ 37·B/(36 − c) at
   equal base: the ranking at equal B grows with coverage (§3).
3. **×2 controls for c > 18 under-recover** (two dozens, 25 and 35 straight-ups): they are loss
   chasers, not capped ladders. They reproduce the user's "nothing but even money works" for a
   different reason than the c < 18 controls (which over-recover and cap in 14–33 spins). Keep
   them, exclude them from the e^(−x) fit (`meta.recovers: false`).
4. **35 straight-ups:** kept; L = 2 forced by the $500 straight max; see §4.6. Its ×2 control is
   degenerate and could be dropped without loss; it is cheap, so kept for completeness.
5. **No placement dropped.** The one change from the plan text: the extra `-u5` pair on the dozen,
   to show the B/u lever (n½ 765, positive median at 500) next to the placement effect at equal B.
6. The runner prints no `rejected`/`adjusted` counts; at a $5,000 bankroll they are non-zero
   after caps by construction. If the orchestrator wants them in reports, `runOne` can sum
   `out.table.rejected/adjusted` and `out.chunks[].rejectedByReason` (a tooling change, T2/T4's
   file).
