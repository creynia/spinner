# E9: slow-recovery ladders

Serves `docs/research/plan.md` §7.18 (predictions, written before the runs) and §7.19 (results).
Inputs: `results/x-e9-{controls,fib,geo-back,geo-reset,dal-lab,transplant,cashout}.json` (10,000
sessions, seed 20261031), `results/x-e9-holdout.json` (seeds 1 and 2), and the predictions in
`results/x-e9-predictions.json`, produced by `scripts/research/e9.ts` from
`scripts/research/slowLadder.ts` before any run. European wheel, $5,000 bankroll, 500 spins.

**The two questions.** (1) At a fixed bankroll, does a ladder that needs several wins to unwind a
cycle buy a longer median horizon or a higher P(ahead) than the best one-win ladder, and what does it
cost in the tail? (2) Does E5's "layout is no lever beyond bankroll in cycle profits" survive when
cycles need several wins?

**Short answers.** (1) No. Every slow ladder sits on or below the one-win frontier: at the same
bankroll and the same P(win) its median is 11–93 % lower than the one-win ladder's on the same
numbers, because a slow cycle wagers more per dollar
of cycle profit (k = 1.33–4.27 against 1.21–1.38). Fibonacci $5 back 2, the best on red, matches the
$2 Martingale's P(win) (85.9 % vs 85.0 %) with a lower median ($415 vs $478), a worse mean (−$269 vs
−$152) and a worse tail (CVaR 5 % −$4,862 vs −$4,011), and a $1 one-win dozen beats it on all four
on three seeds. Ladders that step back one level per win
(d'Alembert, one-step-back sequences, Fibonacci back 1) are much worse: on red the level drifts up,
so the ladder is lost once every 14–34 cycles whatever its length. (2) Yes for the never-capped share,
with two amendments: the recovery rule is now a first-order lever on k (and on whether the ladder works
at all), and P(ahead) separates from P(no cap) when a win does not clear the deficit.

**Model.** A level-indexed ladder is a Markov chain on the level (loss +1, win → max(0, k − back)). A
cycle runs from level 0 until a win lands on level 0 (done) or a loss on the top level (cap). The chain
gives q, E[T], τ̄ (mean done-cycle profit), C (mean cap loss), turnover per cycle, and from them λ =
q·S/E[T], e^(−λ), n½ = ln 2·E[T]/q, p_eff = (1 − q)/E[T], B = C/τ̄, x = p_eff·S/B and k = qB/(1 − q)
(so e^(−λ) = e^(−k·x)). "Exact" is the chain run for 500 spins; "DP" is the session over (level,
equity) with the harness's reconcile rules. Labouchere (state = the line) uses 10⁶ sampled cycles and
has no exact or DP column. Every slow ladder's maxSteps is the number of levels whose straight losing
run fits $5,000; the `-stop` twin (`onCap: stop`) gives the never-capped share. Stop-win/loss are in
`session`; dozen and c9 slow ladders step back 4 or 5 levels per win through a sequence's `onWin:
back` plus a rule `{ when: { event: 'bet-won' }, then: [{ prog: 'q', do: 'back' }, ...] }`.

## 2. Question 1: horizon and P(ahead) at a fixed bankroll

**Answer: no.** A slow ladder can have a long horizon (Fibonacci $5 back 2: n½ 2,358 spins, against 1,116
for the $5 Martingale), but only because it earns less per spin. At the same bankroll the one-win
ladders reach the same P(win) with a higher median, a smaller mean loss and a tail at least as good.

The mechanism is turnover. By the edge identity, k = 1 + 0.027 × turnover / ((1 − q)·τ̄), and the median
at a given never-capped share P0 is about C·ln(1/P0)/k for a cap loss C. A slow cycle bets more for the
same profit: turnover per dollar of cycle profit is 24.1 for Fibonacci $5 back 2, 13.4 for the dozen
back-4 transplant, 12.3 for c9 back 4, and 121 for Fibonacci back 3. The one-win ladders on the same $5,000
need 11.3–12.6 (Martingale red), 8.6–9.5 (dozen ×1.5) and 7.8 (c9). So k is 1.33–4.27 for the slow ladders
with a positive median and 1.21–1.38 for the one-win ones. The measured efficiency, median ÷ (C·ln(1/P(win))),
is 0.69–0.79 for every one-win ladder and 0.05–0.69 for the slow ones (§1 table and the efficiency table
below).

  (Martingale red), 1.65 (Fibonacci back 2), 4.27 (back 3); 1.22–1.26 (dozen one-win) vs 1.36 / 1.51 (back 4 /

  over more money per dollar of profit. Layout matters for slow ladders in one new way: the back that gives a
  downward drift depends on the payout ((1 − p) − back·p < 0), just as the one-win growth factor does
  (g = 36/(36 − c)). Fibonacci's back 2 on a dozen has drift +1/37 and would be a random walk (DP, not run:
  ×1.36 back 2 on a dozen keeps 2.7 % of sessions uncapped at 500 spins).
- **Amendment 2: the curve predicts the never-capped share, not P(ahead).** For a one-win ladder a survivor
  is always ahead, so P(win) ≈ never-capped and "median > 0 iff e^(−k·x) > ½" (E5) holds. For a ladder that
  under-recovers, a survivor can be deep in an unfinished cycle: Fibonacci $5 back 3 is 95.7 % never capped

  .614, exact .780, median negative) and the closed-form median overstates it by $1,478.
- **The Poisson step needs short cycles.** e^(−k·x) matches the exact chain within .01 for the 20 ladders with

## 6. What it means for the Research screen

- **Chapter 2 ("Why doubling feels like it works")** says Fibonacci "grows more slowly and is still ahead at
  spin 500 (+$1,675)". That is true of the preset, whose 12 levels need $9,400 and whose sessions bust 39.1 %
  of the time on $5,000. Sized to fit $5,000 at $25, Fibonacci caps a cycle as often as Martingale-L8 (.0048)
  and its median at 500 is −$1,150. Suggested text: "Fibonacci's preset stays ahead longer because its 12
  steps are more than $5,000 can pay for: 39 % of sessions bust. Sized to the bankroll, it caps as often as
  Martingale."
- **Chapter 3 ("Levels, not luck")** holds and gets a second condition: levels only buy time if a win moves
  the ladder down faster than losses move it up. Add a takeaway: "Stepping back one level per win (d'Alembert,
  'one step back' ladders) never works on red: losses outnumber wins 19 to 18, so the ladder drifts to the top
  and is lost about once every 14 to 34 cycles, however many steps it has." And: "A ladder that needs several
  wins per loss stays ahead longer only because it earns less per spin; at the same bankroll a one-win ladder
  with a smaller base does better." Formula for the math box: P(no cap) from the level chain, drift (1 − p) −
  back·p < 0.
- **Chapter 5 ("One curve rules them all")** holds as a ceiling (now 31 ladders more, none above it) and as
  a point prediction for short-cycle ladders. Three edits: (1) "the shape of the bet is not the lever" stays,
  but add "the recovery rule is: slow-recovery ladders have k from 1.33 to 4.27 against 1.21 to 1.38 for one-win
  ladders on the same numbers, always the wrong way"; (2) the curve gives the never-capped share; for ladders
  that do not clear the deficit in one win, P(ahead) can be 30 points lower (Fibonacci back 3: 95.7 % vs 65.3 %);
  (3) "the median is positive exactly when e^(−k·x) > ½" should be restricted to one-win ladders (it failed on
  d'Alembert $5).
- **Calculator.** `ladderMath.ts` assumes one-win cycles, q = (1 − p)^L. For a slow ladder it is wrong by
  orders of magnitude (d'Alembert $5: (19/37)^44 ≈ 10⁻¹³ vs q .030). `scripts/research/slowLadder.ts` is
  pure and could back a "back" input; until then the screen should say the calculator covers one-win ladders
  only.
