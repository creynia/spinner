# A3: H1 against E3a/E3b, the renewal correction, and the E7 shortfall

Serves plan.md §2 H1 and §4 E3/E7; HANDOFF.md §7 wave 1 "A3". Inputs: `results/e3a-levels.json`,
`e3b-lengths.json`, `e7-mart.json` (seed 20261031, 10,000 sessions), and reconcile's rules in
`src/game/roulette/reconcile.ts` (read only).

**Reference model used throughout ("DP").** An exact forward recursion over (equity in base
units, ladder level) with the harness's rules: the bet is min(2^k, equity). Reconcile step 6
(`shortfall: 'reduce'`) cuts an unaffordable bet down to the remaining equity if that still meets
the table minimum, and rejects it as `unaffordable` (the session busts) only when equity is below
the minimum. stopWin and stopLoss are checked on equity after each round. No sampling, no fitted
parameter. It reproduces every E3a/E3b/E7 row inside the measured 95 % CI: P(win), median
(exactly, in units), bust and mean. The closed forms below are judged against it and against the
measurements. Notation: p = 18/37, q = (1 − p)^L, B = 2^L − 1 units, λ = q·p·S.

## 1. Checkpoint spacing: H1 needs no correction

The path is sampled every spin up to 50 and every 10 spins after that, so `medianAheadUntil` = m
means the true crossing lies in [m, m + 10). Every n½ that falls inside a session lands in its
cell, except L10-S2000, which misses by 2.5 spins. The sampling error of the crossing,
SE ≈ 0.005 / (½·q·p), is 16 spins there, so the miss is noise: the measured ahead-share is
0.5002 at spin 1120. Interpolating the ahead-share between checkpoints puts every crossing
within 1.6 SE of ln2/(q·p). The exact no-cap horizon (the first n at which P(no run of L losses)
< ½) differs from the formula by at most one spin. Renewal (item 2) cannot shift n½. A capped
session can be ahead again only after it has banked a whole ladder, which takes about (B+1)/p
spins, and n½·p/(B+1) = ln2/(38/37)^L < 0.7 for every L. So the median crosses zero before any
renewal is possible. Note: the three L8 rows (e3a-L8, e3b-L8-S1000, e3b-L8-S2000) share seed and
paths up to spin 500, so they confirm the horizon once, not three times.

## 2. Renewal: P(ahead at S) when a cap does not end the session

H1 predicts P(never capped by S) ≈ e^(−λ). The ledger's P(win) is P(ahead at S), which also
counts sessions that capped and then came back. With `onCap: reset` a cap costs B units. In E3
the bankroll is exactly B units. A session that has banked n cycles therefore holds equity n
after a cap. If n > B it is still ahead and keeps playing full ladders. If n < B its ladder is
truncated. A truncated Martingale is a near-fair game, so climbing back to B+1 before ruin has
probability ≈ Π_{x=n}^{B} (1 − 1/(x+1)) = n/(B+1). That gives a one-parameter chain over cycles
c, with equity e in units:

```
F_c(e) = (1 − q_e)·F_{c−1}(e+1) + q_e·[e ≥ B]·F_{c−1}(e − B),   F_0(e) = [e > B]
q_e = q if e ≥ B (full ladder),  1/(e+1) if e < B (truncated ladder: a "cap" there is ruin)
P(ahead at S) ≈ Σ_w Bin(w; S, p) · F_{round(w/(1−q))}(B)
```

The term beyond e^(−λ) only matters when p·S > B+1, i.e. when the session is long enough to bank
a whole ladder before it caps: L5–L7 at 500 spins and L8 at 1,000 and 2,000. L8-S500 sits on the
edge (p·S = 243 against 256) and picks up +2.5 points through the spread of the win count. The
naive Poisson sum without ruin, P(Poisson(λ) < p·S/B), lets equity go negative and overshoots by
up to 20 points. The chain is 0.1 to 1.7 points high. The residual is the edge paid during
truncated-ladder play, which the fair-game 1/(e+1) ignores.

## 3. E7 shortfall: the 8th bet is reduced, not refused

With $25 units and a $5,000 bankroll, seven losses from profit P (in units) leave equity 73 + P.
The 8th bet wants 128 units, and reconcile reduces it to 73 + P. It is never refused, because
73 + P ≥ 1 unit. Refusal (`unaffordable`, then bust) only happens at equity 0. This matches the
orchestrator's counts: 254 rejected (one per busted session) and 499 adjusted rounds. Losing the
reduced bet busts the session, with the same probability as a cap, so q itself is unchanged.
Winning it does not recover the ladder. Equity becomes 2(73 + P), net P − 54: a setback of
Δ = 55 − P cycles instead of the usual +1. That happens with probability s = p(1 − p)^7 = 0.00455
per cycle, about as often as a cap, and each setback adds roughly q·Δ of cap risk. Summing over
the profit levels a session passes through on its way to W:

```
P(win) ≈ exp(−q·W − s·q·Σ_{P=0}^{min(W,55)−1} (55 − P)),   q_eff = q + s·q·ΣΔ/W
```

q_eff = 0.0060 / 0.0059 / 0.0058 / 0.0055 / 0.0051 for W = 5 / 10 / 20 / 50 / 100. This is the
falling q_eff the E7 agent backed out. It falls because a session above 55 units of profit can
fund the full 8th bet. Reset and stop give identical P(win) up to W = 50. At W = 100 they differ
by 0.1 point, because a session above 55 units that caps keeps playing under reset. The SL-half
rows behave as the ledger says: −$3,187.50 lies below the 7-loss drawdown of −$3,175, so the stop
fires only after the bust (labelled stop-loss, bust 0 %, CVaR still −$5,000).

## 4. Recommendations

**Stop-loss.** A session-equity stop-loss cannot fix E7. A loss stop of $3,000 fires after the
7th loss only if the run starts with profit P ≤ 7 units ($175). Above that, the reduced 8th bet
still fires, so bust and CVaR barely move (W20: P(win) .860, median $500, mean −$110,
CVaR 5 % −$5,000, P(−$5,000) 5.3 %). The stop has to follow the ladder. Use a **trailing
stop-loss of $3,000 from the session high** (any value in ($1,575, $3,175] behaves the same). In
Martingale every win sets a new high, so it fires exactly at the 7th loss of a cycle. It is
identical to `maxSteps: 7, onCap: 'stop'` (HANDOFF §4; strategy.md R16), which is the easier spec.
Its P(win) is the plain e^(−q₇W), with q₇ = (19/37)^7 = 0.00937 per cycle. Predicted with the DP,
500 spins, $5,000:

| variant | W (units) | P(win) | median | mean | CVaR 5 % | P(−$5,000) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| no SL (current E7) | 20 | .885 | $500 | −$130 | −$5,000 | 11.4 % |
| SL $3,000 on equity | 20 | .860 | $500 | −$110 | −$5,000 | 5.3 % |
| trailing $3,000 ≡ L7 onCap stop | 5 / 10 / 20 / 50 / 100 | .954 / .910 / .828 / .623 / .388 | $125 / $250 / $500 / $1,250 / −$1,350 | −$25 / −$49 / −$94 / −$205 / −$333 | −$2,879 / −$3,120 / −$3,120 / −$3,120 / −$3,120 | 0 % |

The trade is the usual skew trade, made visible: at W = 20 it gives up 5.7 points of P(win) and
in exchange removes the $5,000 bust (CVaR −$5,000 → −$3,120) and improves the mean by about $36
through lower turnover. The median stays at +W while q₇·W < ln 2, i.e. W ≤ 73. At W = 100 the
median flips to −$1,350. Use these as the E7-on-winners predictions. For other ladders the rule
is general: the stop must sit at or below the drawdown after the last level the bankroll fully
funds, measured from the session high, not from the start.

**Report a "never capped" share.** Yes. It is the quantity that H1 (e^(−q·p·S)) and H3 (e^(−x))
predict. P(win) mixes it with post-cap recoveries: L8-S2000 shows .212 measured against .008
never capped, and L5 shows .167 against .0001. Until the runner records caps per session, an
exact proxy needs no code change. Run an `onCap: 'stop'` twin on the same seed: its P(win) is
P(never capped and ahead) ≈ P(never capped). That is exact in E3, where the bankroll always funds
the full ladder before the first cap. In E5, plot that twin's share against e^(−x), and keep
P(win) as the second column, with the renewal chain above as its prediction.
